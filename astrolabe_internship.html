Overview

The Project

Through a professor-led initiative, I joined a red teaming team tasked with stress-testing AI Astrolabe's NOVA model — a large language model deployed on AWS — for unsafe or harmful outputs. Our angle was unique: we probed the model exclusively through Urdu-language prompts, targeting potential blind spots in multilingual safety guardrails that English-only testing would miss.

"If the model held safe, we adapted — escalating prompting strategies until we either elicited an unsafe response or confirmed the guardrail was robust."

Every result — the prompt, the model's response, and a safe/unsafe label — was logged in a shared Excel sheet, building a structured dataset of adversarial test cases for the AI Astrolabe team.

Methodology

How We Tested

1

Initial Prompt Design

Crafted adversarial prompts in Urdu targeting categories known to elicit unsafe outputs — harmful instructions, sensitive topics, manipulative framing — leveraging nuances of Urdu phrasing that differ from English equivalents.

urdu-nlp
2

Model Interaction on AWS

Submitted prompts to the NOVA model via AWS, observed responses in full, and assessed whether the output crossed safety thresholds — considering both explicit and implied harmful content.

aws · nova-model
3

Iterative Strategy Escalation

When the model stayed safe, the prompt wasn't simply discarded — it triggered a strategy pivot. We modified framing, injected roleplay contexts, used indirect phrasing, or chained prompts to try to elicit unsafe behaviour through a different angle.

adversarial-prompting
4

Logging & Labeling

Each prompt-response pair was recorded in a shared Excel sheet with a safe/unsafe label. As reviewer, I audited these entries for label consistency, catching misclassifications and ensuring the dataset's integrity.

data-labeling · qa

Reflection

Key Takeaways

🌐

Multilingual Safety Gaps Are Real

Testing in Urdu revealed that safety guardrails optimised for English can behave differently with other languages — a genuinely underexplored area in AI safety.

⬆️

Rapid Trust & Promotion

Being moved from labeler to reviewer within half a day reinforced that attention to detail and consistent judgment are immediately visible in collaborative data work.

🔄

Adversarial Thinking Is a Skill

Learning to pivot prompting strategy when an attempt fails — rather than stopping — taught a systematic, iterative approach to security testing that extends beyond AI.

🤝

Data Quality Is a Team Effort

Reviewing others' labels made clear how subjective "safe/unsafe" can be at the margins — and how critical clear guidelines and cross-checking are to dataset integrity.
